Two properties govern everything downstream: the archive stores exact integers (§2), so no precision is invented or lost before the maths begins; and every stage banks its progress (§9), so a kill costs minutes, never hours.

§2 · From a row to a picture

From a published element to something you can see

Space-Track publishes fitted orbital elements. We store them as quantised integers and turn them into geometry. Here is one real transformation, end to end.

A stored row holds mean_motion_q = 1544218077. The scale for mean motion is 10−8 rev/day, so:

n = 1544218077 × 10−8 = 15.44218077 rev/day = 1.122987 × 10−3 rad/s

Kepler's third law converts a rate you can count into a distance you can draw. With the gravitational parameter the pipeline uses, μ = 398 600.8 km³/s² (WGS-72, matching the element sets' own model):

a = μn21/3 = 398 600.8(1.122987 × 10−3)21/3 = 6 811.8 km → altitude ≈ 434 km

The steps are chosen so the stored integer is exact for every value Space-Track actually publishes — not approximately right, bit-for-bit right. That is what lets §8's column store promise byte-identical round-trips, and lets two independent compute paths (CPU and GPU, §7) be compared flag-for-flag instead of "close enough".

§3 · The step detector

What a manoeuvre looks like in the data

Drag pulls an orbit down smoothly. A burn moves it in one step. The whole detector is a careful way of asking: is this change too big to be noise, against this object's own recent behaviour?

The recipe, each constant verified in pipeline/orbit_campaigns.py:

  • Blocks of 5 days (BLOCK_DAYS, L145): 5–15 fits per block for a routinely tracked object, so the block median shrugs off any single bad fit.
  • Baseline over ±6 blocks (L153) — 60 days. A median's breakdown point is 50%: an object would have to be manoeuvring in 7 of 13 blocks before its own burns polluted its baseline.
  • Robust scale = MAD × 1.4826 (L557) — the outlier-proof stand-in for a standard deviation.
  • Trip rule: |residual| ≥ κ·scale with κ = 8 (L164).
  • Persistence (L199): the change must survive 2 more days — several further independent fits — or it was a fitting artefact, not a burn.
trip ⇔ | ablock − median(±6 blocks) | ≥ 8 × 1.4826 × MAD and the step persists ≥ 2 days

§4 · Pricing the burn

Pricing the burn, honestly

We never see the burn — only the orbit before and after. So every published cost is the cheapest manoeuvre consistent with the observed change: a lower bound. Any real burn in another direction, at another point, cost more.

Δvtangential = ½ · n · Δa   ·   Δvplane = 2V · sin(Δi/2)   ·   Δvshape = ½ · V · Δe

Worked example at the §2 orbit (n = 1.123×10⁻³ rad/s): raising the semi-major axis by 10 km costs at least ½ × 1.123×10⁻³ × 10 000 m = 5.6 m/s. That is a routine constellation raise — and exactly the size of step Fig. 3 catches.

The 2V ceiling — physics as a data filter

No single impulse can change a bound orbit by more than twice the speed at perigee — that is the cost of stopping the satellite dead and reversing it. Any implied Δv above 2V is therefore not an expensive manoeuvre; it is two element sets that do not describe the same object (a mis-identification or a bad refit).

§5 · The free blank sample

How the detector proves itself

Debris and spent rocket stages cannot manoeuvre. Every flag the detector raises on one is, on the strength of the catalogue, a false alarm. That is a free, always-on blank — 89 million intervals of it — and it is re-measured on every complete sweep.

From the live artifact (11 Sep 2026): 39 924 flags on 89 201 284 passive intervals. The rate alone is not enough — we publish the Jeffreys 95% upper bound, so sampling luck cannot flatter us:

rate = 39 924 / 89 201 284 = 4.476 × 10⁻⁴  ·  upper95 = Beta(k+½, n−k+½)0.975 = 4.52 × 10⁻⁴ < 10⁻³ target ✓

Why an effect size and not a p-value: with 89M + 63M intervals, a two-proportion test is "significant" at a rate ratio of 1.001 — it measures the size of the archive, not the skill of the detector. The 10× bound-against-bound requirement (orbit_events.py L2564) cannot be satisfied by a detector that met its false-alarm target by detecting nothing.

§6 · The long-arc lane

The drift lane: three failures, then a detector

The step detector is blind to slow change — a 60-day median absorbs a gradual trend into its own baseline. A separate lane hunts trends. Its first three designs failed their own blank, and the failures pointed straight at the answer.

v4 — one sign, one gate, no drag model

Drag can only pull an orbit down. Below 800 km, near-circular, nothing without an engine sustains a climb. So v4 flags exactly one thing: a significantly positive semi-major-axis slope inside the gate perigee < 800 km e < 0.05 (orbit_drift.py L303, threshold z ≥ 5 at L93).

§7 · The hardware argument

Why GPUs, and how the numbers get there

Profiled on the real code path, the sweep is 92.6% arithmetic, 7.4% reading (11 Sep 2026). The arithmetic is the same small formula applied 89 million times — exactly what a GPU is for. The point is not sweep speed. It is giving 12 CPU cores back to everything else this machine serves.

mode (same 1 000-object slice, 12 Sep)wallCPU core-min
CPU only, single reader51.18 s0.852

verify (runs both, compares)111.56 s1.740

Scaled to production: the sweep's CPU cost falls from roughly 190 to ~15 core-minutes a day (extrapolation, so labelled), at the price of longer wall time inside slices the 20-hour publish cadence never notices. If the card is unavailable, the run says so on stderr and completes on CPU — slower, never different.

§8 · Feeding the card without burning the CPU

Columnization

Even with the maths on the GPU, the CPU was still re-parsing 216M SQLite rows and re-dividing every integer, every sweep — work that never changes. The column store does it once, and the answer is memory-mapped arrays a device copy can take directly.

The integrity contract is the hard part. The archive is not append-only — back-fills and monthly rollups rewrite history — so a derived store that quietly disagreed with SQLite would be worse than a slow one. The store verifies per-object counts and content hashes against the archive and refuses to serve on any failure (a full rebuild path exists). Dequantisation is checked to round-trip the stored integer exactly, or it raises. Analysis stays stateless: the store caches the representation, never a result. Integration into the sweep is landing now.

§9 · Sharing the machine

Being a good tenant

bigmem also serves the weather site and hosts resident models on the GPUs. Every design choice above is bent around one rule: this pipeline must never be the reason something else is slow.

The spare-VRAM broker

Sean's rule for the cards: models that live resident own their VRAM; everything transient queues for what is left (≈1.5–1.9 GB per card when the residents are loaded). A request declares its size; the broker admits it onto whichever card fits it — keeping a 512 MiB reserve untouched for the residents — or queues it, priority first with aging so nothing starves. On contention a job waits; it never flees to CPU, because on this machine CPU is the scarcer resource. Every session writes a receipt, mirrored to /brain/gpu, so unmeasured GPU use — a defect by estate rule — cannot happen quietly.

§10 · The honesty appendix

What is claimed, what is not, and when each number was measured

  • Every Δv is a lower bound. The cheapest manoeuvre consistent with the observed change; real burns cost more. "At least 12 m/s" is a floor, and the useful honest statement.
  • No event was called a manoeuvre, as of the 11 Sep 2026 artifact this page audits. The gate needs the false-alarm bound under 10⁻³ and 10× separation; at 6.76× everything was a candidate. The bar was never moved to pass it — a corroboration fix raised the separation instead, and the gate has since opened for this lane; see the site's public How-it-works page for the current separation.
  • The drift lane claims one narrow thing — sustained raising in the drag regime — and states its blind spots: continuous station-keeping, lowering, everything above 800 km, GEO relocations.
  • A debris flag is "false by the catalogue's own account." A fragment cannot burn, but a catalogue entry can be wrong or stale; the warrant is stated, not overstated.
  • Dropped data is counted. The 2V ceiling reports how many intervals it removed. A bound that deletes silently is a defect.
